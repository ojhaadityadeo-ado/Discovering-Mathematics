OPEN CASE

MATH DETECTIVE: CASE FILE #12

Subject: The Difference-Angle Identities

Mission: Uncover sin(A − B), cos(A − B), and tan(A − B) using the addition identities and the meaning of a negative angle.

Detective Briefing: The formulas for a sum are already in our evidence file. Can we turn a difference into a sum by changing the orientation of one angle?
Exhibit 01 / Reverse the rotation

P = (cos B, sin B)
P′ = (cos B, −sin B)

Same horizontal coordinate.
Opposite vertical coordinate.

Illustration: B is positive and acute. Reflection and the negative-angle identities hold for every real B.

Keep this clue in view
B′ = −B
A − B = A + B′
Negative-angle facts
cos(−B) = cos B
sin(−B) = −sin B
tan(−B) = −tan B

The tangent fact requires cos B ≠ 0.

Clue 01 — Know the target

Can subtraction become addition?

Let A and B be any two real angles. We want to find sin(A − B), cos(A − B), and tan(A − B) using functions of the separate angles.

Our first suspect is sin(A − B). What do we already know? The sine of a sum.

sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B

Detective, can you write A − B as the sum of two angles?

Open the substitution envelope
B′ = −B
A − B = A + (−B) = A + B′

We have changed the form of the expression. Now the addition formula can do the work.

Detective’s Note: We use addition identities already established for real angles. No acute-angle triangle restriction is needed here. Tangent will require a domain check.
Clue 02 — Read the orientation

What does a negative angle mean?

When we defined an angle, we included its orientation: the direction of rotation from the initial ray.

By convention, anticlockwise rotation is positive and clockwise rotation is negative.

The angle −A has the same magnitude as A, with the direction reversed. Likewise, B′ = −B reverses the rotation of B. If B is already negative, −B is positive.

Inspect the unit-circle exhibit. Reversing the angle reflects its endpoint across the horizontal axis: the horizontal coordinate stays the same and the vertical coordinate changes sign.

cos(−B) = cos B
sin(−B) = −sin B

Since tangent is sine divided by cosine, wherever it is defined,

tan(−B) = sin(−B)cos(−B)
= −sin Bcos B = −tan B

Clue 03 — The sine deduction

Find sin(A − B)

Set B′ = −B. The difference is now a sum, so apply the familiar sine addition identity.

sin(A − B) = sin(A + B′)
= sin A cos B′ + cos A sin B′
= sin A cos(−B) + cos A sin(−B)
Evidence to substitute: cos(−B) = cos B and sin(−B) = −sin B.

Open the sine proof
sin(A − B) = sin A cos B + cos A (−sin B)
sin(A − B) = sin A cos B − cos A sin B

The negative sign comes from the oddness of sine. This identity holds for all real A and B.

Clue 04 — The cosine deduction

Find cos(A − B)

Repeat the same move: B′ = −B. First recall the cosine addition identity.

cos(A + B′) = cos A cos B′ − sin A sin B′

Now substitute the angle −B.

cos(A − B) = cos(A + B′)
= cos A cos(−B) − sin A sin(−B)

Clue — Track both negative signs

The addition formula already contains a minus sign. Replacing sin(−B) by −sin B introduces another. What happens when you subtract a negative product?

Open the cosine proof
cos(A − B) = cos A cos B − sin A (−sin B)
cos(A − B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

Two negative signs produce the plus sign. This identity holds for all real A and B.

Clue 05 — The tangent deduction

Find tan(A − B)

Use B′ = −B once more. Recall the tangent addition identity, where its terms and quotient are defined.

tan(A + B′) = tan A + tan B′1 − tan A tan B′

Substitute B′ = −B and use tan(−B) = −tan B.

tan(A − B) = tan A + tan(−B)1 − tan A tan(−B)
= tan A − tan B1 − tan A (−tan B)

Open the tangent proof
tan(A − B) = tan A − tan B1 + tan A tan B

The numerator subtracts tan B; the two negative signs in the denominator give a plus.

Check the domain before closing this case.

The formula written in terms of tan A and tan B requires

cos A ≠ 0,   cos B ≠ 0,
1 + tan A tan B ≠ 0.

Equivalently, A, B, and A − B must each avoid π/2 + kπ, for any integer k. Under the first two conditions, the last condition is exactly cos(A − B) ≠ 0.

Cross-check with the sine and cosine results
tan(A − B) = sin(A − B)cos(A − B)
= sin A cos B − cos A sin Bcos A cos B + sin A sin B

This ratio is valid whenever cos(A − B) ≠ 0. Dividing the numerator and denominator by cos A cos B gives the tangent formula, provided cos A cos B ≠ 0.

Clue 06 — Close the case

One substitution. Three identities.

The common method: Write A − B as A + B′, set B′ = −B, use an addition identity, and apply the negative-angle facts.
Sine
sin(A − B) = sin A cos B − cos A sin B
Cosine
cos(A − B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

The sine and cosine identities hold for all real A and B.

Tangent
tan(A − B) = tan A − tan B1 + tan A tan B

For the tangent formula: cos A ≠ 0, cos B ≠ 0, and cos(A − B) ≠ 0.

One last trap — Can tan(A − B) exist when tan A does not?

Yes. Take A = π/2 and B = π/4. Then tan(A − B) = tan(π/4) = 1, but tan A is undefined. The formula written using tan A and tan B cannot be used for this pair.

Case Closed: Reversing the second angle turns our known addition identities into difference identities. Every sign has an explanation.
