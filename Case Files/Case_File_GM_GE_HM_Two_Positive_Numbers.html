MATH DETECTIVE: CASE FILE

Subject: GM ≥ HM for Two Positive Numbers

Mission: Starting from the known inequality \(A\ge G\) and the relation \(G^2=AH\), uncover why the harmonic mean must lie below the geometric mean, why reciprocals reverse the comparison, and exactly when all three means coincide.

The case: For positive real numbers \(a,b\), define \(A=\frac{a+b}{2}\), \(G=\sqrt{ab}\), and \(H=\frac{2ab}{a+b}\). We already know \(A\ge G\). Our task is to prove \(G\ge H\) and then combine the two inequalities.

Follow the clues in order. The questions are designed to make each algebraic move feel inevitable: rewrite, compare, take reciprocals carefully, and trace equality back to the original numbers.

Act 1: Put the three means on the table

For \(a,b>0\),

\[ A=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad G=\sqrt{ab},\qquad H=\frac{2ab}{a+b}. \]

We already have one part of the chain:

\[A\ge G.\]

What is still missing?

Clue 1 · Which comparison must we prove next?

Follow this clue: The desired chain is \(A\ge G\ge H\).

Small step 1. Why can we safely say \(A>0\), \(G>0\), and \(H>0\)?

Open the answer

Because \(a,b>0\). Thus \(a+b>0\), \(ab>0\), and \(\sqrt{ab}>0\). Positivity will matter when we take reciprocals and multiply inequalities.

Act 2: Discover the bridge relation

Instead of comparing \(G\) and \(H\) directly, multiply \(A\) and \(H\):

\[ AH=\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\left(\frac{2ab}{a+b}\right). \]
Clue 2 · What cancels immediately?

Follow this clue: Numerator and denominator contain the same positive factors.

So

\[AH=ab.\]

But

\[G^2=(\sqrt{ab})^2=ab.\]

Therefore

\[\boxed{G^2=AH}.\]

Small step 2. What becomes possible once we know \(G^2=AH\)?

Open the answer

We can express one mean in terms of the other two. In particular, solving for \(H\) gives a form containing \(A\) in the denominator, which fits naturally with the known comparison \(A\ge G\).

Act 3: Rewrite H in a useful form

From

\[G^2=AH,\]

divide by \(A\), which is positive:

\[\boxed{H=\frac{G^2}{A}}.\]
Clue 3 · Which known inequality now interacts directly with this formula?

Follow this clue: The denominator is A, and A is already compared with G.

Small step 3. Why is writing \(H=G^2/A\) more useful here than leaving \(AH=G^2\)?

Open the answer

It turns the problem into comparing two quotients with the same positive numerator \(G^2\): one divided by A and one divided by G.

Act 4: Take reciprocals carefully

We know

\[A\ge G>0.\]

For positive quantities, taking reciprocals reverses the inequality:

\[\frac1A\le\frac1G.\]
Clue 4 · Why does the inequality reverse?

Follow this clue: Compare \(1/5\) with \(1/3\).

Small step 4. What would go wrong if we ignored the sign of A and G?

Open the answer

The reciprocal rule depends on sign. For positive numbers the order reverses cleanly; with negative or mixed signs, one must analyze the situation separately.

Small step 5. If \(A=G\), what happens to their reciprocals?

Open the answer

They are equal: \(1/A=1/G\). So equality is preserved through this reciprocal step.

Act 5: Multiply by the common positive factor

From

\[\frac1A\le\frac1G,\]

multiply both sides by \(G^2>0\):

\[ \frac{G^2}{A}\le\frac{G^2}{G}. \]
Clue 5 · What should we simplify next?

Follow this clue: Use \(H=G^2/A\) and \(G>0\).

Therefore

\[H\le G.\]

Equivalently,

\[\boxed{G\ge H}.\]

Small step 6. Why does multiplying by \(G^2\) not reverse the inequality?

Open the answer

Because \(G^2>0\). Multiplication by a positive quantity preserves the direction of an inequality.

Act 6: Assemble the full chain

We already knew

\[A\ge G,\]

and we have now proved

\[G\ge H.\]

So by transitivity,

\[\boxed{A\ge G\ge H}.\]
Clue 6 · What does transitivity contribute here?

Follow this clue: If A is at least G and G is at least H, compare A and H.

Act 7: Equality and stress-tests

We know from AM ≥ GM that

\[A=G\iff a=b.\]

If \(a=b=c>0\), then

\[ A=\frac{c+c}{2}=c,\qquad G=\sqrt{c^2}=c,\qquad H=\frac{2c^2}{2c}=c. \]

Hence

\[A=G=H.\]
Clue 7 · Can G=H occur when A>G?

Follow this clue: Trace strict inequality through the reciprocal and multiplication steps.

Therefore

\[\boxed{A=G=H\iff a=b}.\]

Stress-test A · Equal inputs

Take \(a=b=8\). Then

\[A=8,\qquad G=8,\qquad H=8.\]

Stress-test B · Unequal inputs

Take \(a=4,b=9\). Then

\[ A=\frac{13}{2}=6.5,\qquad G=6,\qquad H=\frac{72}{13}\approx5.538. \]

Indeed,

\[6.5>6>\frac{72}{13}.\]

Small step 8. Why are these numerical checks useful?

Open the answer

They show both possible behaviors predicted by the proof: equality for equal inputs and strict inequalities for unequal positive inputs.

Final case report

For positive real numbers \(a,b\),

\[ A=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad G=\sqrt{ab},\qquad H=\frac{2ab}{a+b}. \]

First,

\[AH=ab=G^2.\]

Hence

\[H=\frac{G^2}{A}.\]

Since \(A\ge G>0\),

\[\frac1A\le\frac1G.\]

Multiplying by \(G^2>0\),

\[\frac{G^2}{A}\le\frac{G^2}{G},\]

so

\[\boxed{G\ge H}.\]

Combining with AM ≥ GM,

\[\boxed{A\ge G\ge H}.\]

Equality throughout occurs exactly when \(a=b\).
