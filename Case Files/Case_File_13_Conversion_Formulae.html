OPEN CASE

MATH DETECTIVE: CASE FILE #13

Subject: Conversion Formulae

Mission: Convert products into sums and differences, then use that discovery to convert sums and differences into products.

Detective Briefing: Familiar terms are hiding in known identities. Find them, cancel what you do not need, and discover how the same evidence solves the reverse problem.
Evidence board / Known identities
Sine: sumsin(A + B) =
sin A cos B + cos A sin B
Sine: differencesin(A − B) =
sin A cos B − cos A sin B
Cosine: sumcos(A + B) =
cos A cos B − sin A sin B
Cosine: differencecos(A − B) =
cos A cos B + sin A sin B
The detective’s question

Where have I seen this term—or something similar—before?

Cancellation tools

(X + Y) + (X − Y) = 2X

(X + Y) − (X − Y) = 2Y

First recognise the pattern.
Then choose the operation.
Clue 01 — List the possibilities

Which products need investigating?

Part I / Products into sums or differences

Choose sine or cosine for angle A, and sine or cosine for angle B. What products can we make?

sin A sin B,   sin A cos B,   cos A cos B

Open the fourth possibility
cos A sin B = sin B cos A

Sine and cosine values are real numbers, and multiplication of real numbers is commutative: xy = yx. We can therefore reverse the order of the factors.

Once we know the formula for sin A cos B, we can interchange A and B to obtain the formula for sin B cos A.

Watch the labels: cos A sin B equals sin B cos A. It does not generally equal sin A cos B. Swapping factors and swapping angle labels are different steps.
Three main investigations: sin A sin B, cos A cos B, and sin A cos B. The fourth follows by interchanging A and B.

Have we seen these products before? Search the four addition and difference identities on the evidence board.

Clue 02 — Spot and cancel

First suspect: sin A sin B

The product appears in the two cosine identities. Write them together and inspect their signs.

cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B
cos(A − B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

Our target is sin A sin B. The shared term cos A cos B is in the way. Which operation will cancel it?

Clue — Choose the subtraction order

Subtract cos(A + B) from cos(A − B). This leaves the sine product with a positive coefficient.

Open the first deduction
cos(A − B) − cos(A + B)
= (cos A cos B + sin A sin B)
− (cos A cos B − sin A sin B)
= 2 sin A sin B

Divide both sides by 2:

sin A sin B =
12[cos(A − B) − cos(A + B)]
Detective’s Note: We chose subtraction for a reason: it cancels the common cosine product and doubles the sine product.
Clue 03 — Use the same evidence

Second suspect: cos A cos B

Keep the same pair of cosine identities. This time, we want to remove the sine product.

cos(A − B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B
cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B

Open the cosine-product deduction
cos(A − B) + cos(A + B)
= (cos A cos B + sin A sin B)
+ (cos A cos B − sin A sin B)
= 2 cos A cos B

Divide by 2:

cos A cos B =
12[cos(A − B) + cos(A + B)]
One pair, two discoveries. Subtraction isolated sin A sin B. Addition isolates cos A cos B.
Clue 04 — Follow the mixed products

Third suspect—and the fourth

For sin A cos B, inspect the sine addition and difference identities.

sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
sin(A − B) = sin A cos B − cos A sin B

Open the mixed-product deduction
sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)
= 2 sin A cos B
sin A cos B =
12[sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)]

The fourth product: interchange A and B

By commutativity, cos A sin B = sin B cos A. Apply the formula just found with the angle labels interchanged:

cos A sin B = sin B cos A
= 12[sin(B + A) + sin(B − A)]
Recall: B + A = A + B, while B − A = −(A − B). Sine changes sign when its angle is negated.
sin(B − A) = −sin(A − B)
cos A sin B =
12[sin(A + B) − sin(A − B)]
Cross-check — Subtract the sine identities
sin(A + B) − sin(A − B)
= 2 cos A sin B

This gives the same fourth formula directly.

Clue 05 — Recognise the reverse route

Can the discovery work backwards?

Part II / Sums or differences into products

Our new targets are:

sin C + sin D,   sin C − sin D
cos C + cos D,   cos C − cos D

It may not be obvious how to begin. Ask the detective’s question: Have we just seen something similar?

We have already found a sum of two sines:

sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)
= 2 sin A cos B

The angle inputs are written as A + B and A − B. Could these simply be our new angles C and D?

C = A + B,   D = A − B

Given any real C and D, elementary algebra lets us find A and B. Add the equations to find A; subtract the second from the first to find B.

Open the algebra envelope
C + D = 2A,   C − D = 2B
A = C + D2,   B = C − D2

Substitution verifies A + B = C and A − B = D. These choices work for every pair of real angles C and D.

The bridge: A is the half-sum of C and D; B is the half-difference. The earlier identities already contain the conversions we need.
Clue 06 — Make the substitution

Turn sin C + sin D into a product

Start with the identity we discovered by adding the two sine formulas:

sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)
= 2 sin A cos B

Set C = A + B and D = A − B. Then

sin C + sin D = 2 sin A cos B

Now replace A by the half-sum and B by the half-difference.

sin C + sin D =
2 sin(C + D2) cos(C − D2)
What changed? We recognised C and D as a sum and a difference of suitable angles. Solving two linear equations made the earlier product identity immediately useful.

Check the substitution
A = 50°,   B = 20°
sin 70° + sin 30° = 2 sin 50° cos 20°

The inputs recombine correctly: 50° + 20° = 70° and 50° − 20° = 30°.

Clue 07 — Repeat the method

The other three conversions

Use the same substitution throughout:

A = C + D2,   B = C − D2

1. Difference of sines

Our fourth product formula gives

sin(A + B) − sin(A − B)
= 2 cos A sin B
sin C − sin D =
2 cos(C + D2) sin(C − D2)

2. Sum of cosines

Our cosine-product formula gives

cos(A + B) + cos(A − B)
= 2 cos A cos B
cos C + cos D =
2 cos(C + D2) cos(C − D2)

3. Difference of cosines

The first product formula had cos(A − B) minus cos(A + B). Reverse that subtraction to match cos C − cos D:

cos(A + B) − cos(A − B)
= −2 sin A sin B

Open the final conversion
cos C − cos D =
−2 sin(C + D2) sin(C − D2)
Keep the order: The half-difference is (C − D)/2. With this order, cos C − cos D has a negative sign in front.
Clue 08 — Close the case

The complete conversion file

Our discovery route: Recognise familiar products → add or subtract to cancel → reverse the result → replace A and B by the half-sum and half-difference.

Products into sums or differences

sin A sin B =
12[cos(A − B) − cos(A + B)]
cos A cos B =
12[cos(A − B) + cos(A + B)]
sin A cos B =
12[sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)]
cos A sin B =
12[sin(A + B) − sin(A − B)]

Sums or differences into products

sin C + sin D =
2 sin(C + D2) cos(C − D2)
sin C − sin D =
2 cos(C + D2) sin(C − D2)
cos C + cos D =
2 cos(C + D2) cos(C − D2)
cos C − cos D =
−2 sin(C + D2) sin(C − D2)

All eight identities hold for all real angles. Use one consistent angle unit throughout.

Open the final check

Both differences on the left are zero. On the right, the half-difference is zero, and sin 0 = 0, so both products vanish.

Case Closed: The second family was hidden inside the first. Recognising the pattern and changing the angle variables revealed it.
