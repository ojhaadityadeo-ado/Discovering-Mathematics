Subject: The Sine of a Compound Angle

Mission: Start from the familiar right-triangle meaning of sine and uncover a formula for $\sin(A+B)$ using only geometry and ratios we already know.

Detective Briefing: We already know that in a right triangle, \[ \sin\theta=\frac{\text{perpendicular}}{\text{hypotenuse}}. \] Now the question is: how can we express $\sin(A+B)$ using $\sin A$, $\cos A$, $\sin B$, and $\cos B$?

Clue 01 — Know the target

What exactly are we trying to prove?

We know how to find the sine of one angle in a right triangle. For example, \[ \sin A=\frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{hypotenuse}}. \] Now we want a formula for the sine of a sum of two angles, written only in terms of the separate angles $A$ and $B$.
Target: \[ \boxed{\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B}. \]

Our first idea is simple: if we want $\sin(A+B)$, why not draw a right triangle whose one acute angle is $A+B$?

Detective's Note: In a right triangle, the other two angles must both be acute.

Clue 02 — Build the scene

Split $A+B$ into $A$ and $B$

The large triangle contains the angle $A+B$, but our target formula uses $A$ and $B$ separately. So the next step is almost forced: split the compound angle into the two angles we already know.

  1. Inside $\angle QOM$, draw the ray $OP$ so that $\angle POM=A$ and $\angle QOP=B$.
  2. From $Q$, draw $QP\perp OP$.
  3. From $P$, draw $PN\perp OM$.
  4. Through $P$, draw $PR\parallel OM$ until it meets $QM$ at $R$.
Now look for familiar right triangles. The construction gives us \[ \triangle ONP,\qquad \triangle OPQ,\qquad \triangle QRP. \] It also gives a rectangle $MNPR$.
Clue 03 — Collect familiar ratios

What are $\sin A$, $\cos A$, $\sin B$, and $\cos B$ here?

Use $\triangle ONP$ for angle $A$ and $\triangle OPQ$ for angle $B$. Type the ratios using segment names such as PN/OP.

Request a clue

First find the right angle. The side opposite that right angle is the hypotenuse.

Open the ratio evidence envelope
\[ \sin A=\frac{PN}{OP},\qquad \cos A=\frac{ON}{OP}, \] \[ \sin B=\frac{PQ}{OQ},\qquad \cos B=\frac{OP}{OQ}. \]
Clue 04 — Chase the hidden angle

What is $\angle RQP$?

This angle is important because $\triangle QRP$ will give us one more useful ratio.

Clue 1 — Use the parallel lines

Since $PR\parallel OM$, we get $\angle RPO=A$. Since $QP\perp OP$, what is $\angle RPQ$?

Clue 2 — Finish inside $\triangle QRP$

$\angle QRP=\frac\pi2$ and $\angle RPQ=\frac\pi2-A$. What angle remains at $Q$?

Open the angle evidence envelope
\[ \angle RQP=\pi-\frac\pi2-\left(\frac\pi2-A\right)=A. \] Therefore, in $\triangle QRP$, \[ \cos A=\frac{QR}{PQ}. \]
Clue 05 — Assemble the evidence

Follow the vertical height $QM$

Return to the original large right triangle. By definition,

\[ \sin(A+B)=\frac{QM}{OQ}. \]

Now split the height at $R$:

\[ QM=QR+RM. \]

Because $MNPR$ is a rectangle, $RM=PN$. Hence

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin(A+B)&=\frac{QR+PN}{OQ}\\[6pt] &=\frac{PN}{OQ}+\frac{QR}{OQ}. \end{aligned} \]
These last two ratios are not yet among the ratios we know. So we create familiar ratios by multiplying and dividing by a useful intermediate side.

Open the final deduction
\[ \begin{aligned} \sin(A+B)&=\frac{PN}{OP}\cdot\frac{OP}{OQ}\\[6pt] &\quad+\frac{QR}{PQ}\cdot\frac{PQ}{OQ}. \end{aligned} \] Therefore, \[ \boxed{\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B}. \]
What happened geometrically? The total vertical height $QM$ is made of two smaller vertical pieces. Each piece becomes one product in the final formula.

At this stage, the geometric proof has established the result for $A>0$, $B>0$, and $A+B<\pi/2$.

Compare with a complete proof — after your attempt

1. First cover all $0\le a,b<\pi/2$.

The geometric proof gives the formula when $a,b>0$ and $a+b<\pi/2$. Boundary cases with $a=0$ or $b=0$ follow from $\sin0=0$ and $\cos0=1$.

If $a+b=\pi/2$, then $b=\pi/2-a$, so

\[ \sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b =\sin^2a+\cos^2a=1 =\sin\frac\pi2. \]

If $a+b>\pi/2$, let $u=\pi/2-a$ and $v=\pi/2-b$. Then $u+v<\pi/2$ and $a+b=\pi-(u+v)$. Using the acute case plus complementary and supplementary-angle identities gives the same formula for $a,b$.

2. Shift to arbitrary real angles.

For any real $A,B$, choose integers $m,n$ and $a,b\in[0,\pi/2)$ such that

\[ A=m\frac\pi2+a,\qquad B=n\frac\pi2+b. \]

The unit-circle shift rules tell us how sine and cosine change under each quarter-turn. The expression

\[ \sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B \]

changes under a quarter-turn in exactly the same way as $\sin(A+B)$. Since the formula is true for $(a,b)$, repeated quarter-turns carry it to $(A,B)$.
