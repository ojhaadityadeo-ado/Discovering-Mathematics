The Pythagorean Viewpoint

a² + b² = c²

In a first-quadrant unit-circle right triangle, a = cos(x), b = sin(x), and c = 1.

In every quadrant, the side lengths are a = |cos(x)| and b = |sin(x)|. Squaring removes the signs.

Squaring the Visual

A square built on a side of length s has area s × s = s².

|cos(x)| × |cos(x)|→ cos²(x)
|sin(x)| × |sin(x)|→ sin²(x)
1 × 1→ 1

Each colored region is an actual square built outward from its triangle side. Its area changes with the side length.

Synthesis · The identity

sin²(x)Red area+cos²(x)Green area=1Blue area

Pythagoras equates the sum of the squares on the two legs with the square on the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is always the unit radius, so sin²(x) + cos²(x) = 1 for every real angle x. At the axes, one leg has length zero and the other has length one; the identity still holds.
