One value. The same factor twice.

At t = 0, the equation factors as

x² − 4x + 4 = (x − 2)(x − 2) = 0.

The factor (x − 2) occurs twice, so 2 is a root of multiplicity 2, or a repeated root. There is only one distinct real root and one x-intercept. The parabola touches the axis and turns back without crossing.

More generally, for a fixed nonzero a, the separation of two real roots is √Δ / |a|. As Δ → 0⁺, that separation tends to 0. At Δ = 0, f(x) = a(x − r)², where r = −b/(2a).

We use y = a(x − 1)² − 2 to keep the vertex at (1, −2) and the graph scale fixed. In expanded form, b = −2a and c = a − 2 also change. This isolates the effect of a on shape.

Why the width changes: in y = a(x − h)² + k, at a fixed vertical distance d > 0 from the vertex along the opening, |x − h| = √(d / |a|). Larger |a| means a smaller horizontal distance, so the curve is narrower. a = 0 is excluded: the equation would no longer be quadratic.

Keep these three ideas together

Roots locate the x-intercepts.Solve f(x) = 0. Real solutions are the x-coordinates where the curve meets the x-axis.

Δ counts the real intersections.Positive: two. Zero: one, with multiplicity 2. Negative: none, but two non-real complex conjugate roots.

a controls opening and width.Its sign gives up or down; its magnitude gives the vertical stretch. The sign of a alone does not determine the root case.
