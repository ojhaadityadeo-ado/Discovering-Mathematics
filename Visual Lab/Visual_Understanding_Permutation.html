Prastara — Arrangement of Objects

Suppose there are r distinct objects with you and you have to arrange them on a table. In how many ways can you do it? Let's explore the structured logic from Ancient Indian Mathematics.

This is a verse from

Saṅgītaratnākara of Śārṅgadeva

Vol. 1: Treatment of svaras | ONE OF THE SEMINAL TEXTS OF INDIAN MUSIC

क्रमं न्यस्य स्वरः स्थाप्यः पूर्वे पूर्वे परादधः ।
स चेदुपरि तत्पूर्वः पुरस्तूंपरिवर्तिनः ॥
मूलक्रमक्रमात् पृष्ठे शेषाः प्रस्तार ईदृशः ।

This verse gives us a way (an algorithm) to list all the possible arrangements systematically in the form of an array.

THE RULE:

(Interpretation)

Initialization: The first thing you do is label the objects as 1, 2, 3... r. These numbers act as their value. In the first row, the objects are kept in increasing order of their value.
The Iteration (i ≥ 2): Look at the previous row starting from the left. Look for the first element that has at least one element of lower value on its left (call it M).
The Replacement: The largest value object among those lesser value objects on its left is written just below M in the new row.
The Rest: All numbers to the right of M's position are written exactly as they were. The remaining objects are placed to the left in ascending order of value. We continue till all objects are in descending order.

In general, we can show that:

N(r) = r × N(r - 1)

= r × (r - 1) × N(r - 2)

= ... = r × (r - 1) × (r - 2) × ... × 2 × N(1)

= 1 × 2 × ... × (r - 1) × r

N(r) = r!

Therefore, the total number of ways to arrange r distinct objects is = r!

एकस्वरादिसंख्या स्यात् क्रमेण प्रतिमूर्च्छनम् ।

This verse from Saṅgītaratnākara states that the number of distinct Tānas that can be made using r svaras is
= 1 × 2 × ... × r-1 × r = r!

A Tāna is a sequence of seven svaras.

The 7 svaras are: Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni

Total arrangements = 7! = 5,040 combinations!
