Complex multiplication as geometry

Scale the length.
Turn the direction.

Multiply a complex number w by z = x + iy. Its length is multiplied by |z|, and its direction turns by the principal argument Arg(z).

One multiplication · two effects
z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)
w → r w → z w

r = |z| = √(x² + y²)  ·  θ = Arg(z)
First scale by r, then rotate by θ about the origin.
For z ≠ 0, −180° < θ ≤ 180°.

—

All rotations and scalings are centred at 0. Scaling and rotation can be done in either order. Angles add modulo 360°; the principal argument uses (−180°, 180°]. If z = 0, every w maps to 0 and Arg(z) is undefined. The zero vector itself has no direction. Displayed decimals are rounded.
