02

Why the vector equation becomes the Cartesian equation

Same condition, written component-by-component
STEP 1 · SUBTRACT POSITION VECTORS

From two points to one displacement.

A has position vector a⃗ = ⟨x₀,y₀,z₀⟩ and P has position vector r⃗ = ⟨x,y,z⟩. Subtract them to get the vector from A to P.

AP⃗ = r⃗ − a⃗
= ⟨x − x₀, y − y₀, z − z₀⟩
STEP 2 · DOT WITH THE NORMAL

Perpendicular means zero dot product.

Every direction lying in the plane is perpendicular to its normal n⃗ = ⟨a,b,c⟩. So P belongs to the plane exactly when AP⃗ has zero normal component.

AP⃗ · n⃗ = 0
⟨x − x₀, y − y₀, z − z₀⟩ · ⟨a,b,c⟩ = 0
STEP 3 · EXPAND THE DOT PRODUCT

Coordinates reveal the familiar plane equation.

Multiply corresponding components and add. No new geometric fact is introduced—the Cartesian form is simply the dot product written out.

a(x − x₀) + b(y − y₀) + c(z − z₀) = 0
⇔ ax + by + cz = ax₀ + by₀ + cz₀
STEP 4 · INTERPRET THE SIGN

The sign tells which side of the plane P occupies.

Let s = (r⃗ − a⃗)·n⃗. Dividing by |n⃗| gives signed perpendicular distance. Positive and negative values lie on opposite sides; zero lies on the plane.

distance = s / |n⃗|
s = 0 ⇔ P is on the plane

Keep these three ideas together

Subtract to get direction.r⃗ − a⃗ is the displacement from a known point A on the plane to the test point P.
